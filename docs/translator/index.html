Translator guide

Translator listens to a speaker and says what they say in another language, live, on any audio output. One Translator, called a lane, per language.

What you need

  • A Windows PC in the hall or the control room, installed by Spectacast Setup and activated in your account.
  • A plan with Translator: its minutes come with the PC's licence, with nothing to set up. Or your own Google Gemini or OpenAI key.
  • An input with the speaker's voice: a microphone, the mixer, a phone through Mic, or a feed over NDI.

The control page

Each lane has its own control page, at http://127.0.0.1:4720 for the first lane on that PC. Pick:

  1. the input: a sound card, or an NDI source such as one participant from Meet;
  2. the output: a sound card, a virtual cable into OBS or the mixer, or Meet's interpreter channel;
  3. the language to speak;

then press Start. The page shows each sentence twice, as heard and as said, while it is spoken.

Engines

The engine decides how Translator sounds and how fast it answers. Google Gemini is the default; OpenAI is the other. The interpreter engines act as a congress interpreter: give them the session's Topic, and a glossary of names and technical terms, one per line, and they use them.

Flagged meaning

While a lane runs, a second model reads what was heard beside what was said and flags in red the places where the translation says something different: a dropped "not", a number, left for right, a name. It changes nothing in what the lane says; it is for whoever watches the lane. The box Warn when the meaning flips turns it off.

In the meeting

To give remote listeners a language in Zoom, set the lane's output to Meet's interpreter channel. Meet joins as that language's interpreter, and listeners pick the language in Zoom. See Meet.

After a restart

A lane remembers what it was doing. After a restart of the PC it comes back with the same input, output and language, and starts again if it was running, trying for ten minutes while a sound card or the network is not there yet.

Minutes

Every minute a lane speaks counts, per language. Plans with Translator include 300 minutes per hall; your account shows how many you have used. See Account and billing.
